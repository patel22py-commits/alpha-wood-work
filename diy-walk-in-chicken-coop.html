DIY Walk-In Chicken Coop: A Complete Guide to Building a Backyard Chicken Coop

Build a Spacious Walk-In Chicken Coop With a Secure Run, Nesting Boxes, and Easy-Access Doors!

📐 Dimensions: 10' L × 8' W × 8' H (305 × 244 × 244 cm)
🟢 Skill Level: Beginner to Intermediate
💰 Budget Range: $280 – $650
🪵 Key Lumber: 2x4, 4x4, 2x6 Framing & 1/2" Hardware Cloth
🐔 Flock Capacity: 6 to 12 Chickens (Expandable)
📐 Complete 10' × 8' × 8' Walk-In Chicken Coop Blueprints with framing cut lists, predator-proof wire mesh layout, external nesting box, and 8-step assembly sequence.

Dreaming of raising backyard chickens and collecting fresh eggs at home? A well-designed walk-in chicken coop can make caring for your flock easier while creating a beautiful, functional addition to your backyard.

Whether you're planning a small backyard setup or a larger chicken enclosure, building your own coop allows you to customize the size, layout, ventilation, nesting areas, and access doors to suit your needs.

In this guide, we'll explore a DIY walk-in chicken coop inspired by a practical wooden design, including suggested dimensions, materials, essential features, construction steps, and helpful tips for creating a comfortable space for your chickens.

Why Build a Walk-In Chicken Coop?

A walk-in chicken coop offers significant advantages over small, low-access "tractor" style hutches:

🧹
Easier Cleaning: A full 8-foot ceiling lets you stand upright, rake pine shavings, scrub roost bars, and change bedding without crawling or straining your lower back.
🥚
Convenient Egg Collection: External drop-down or lift-up nesting box lids let you gather fresh morning eggs from the outside without stepping into the chicken run.
📐
Organized Layout: Distinct zones for an elevated weather-tight roosting house, an airy sunlit predator-proof run, hanging feeders, and ramp ladders.
🏡
Customizable Dimensions: Easily adapt framing lengths from 6' to 12' to accommodate flock sizes ranging from 4 to 15 hens comfortably.
✨
Attractive Appearance: Natural timber framing, black corrugated metal rooflines, and clean wire panels create a gorgeous modern farmhouse aesthetic for any backyard garden.

1. Walk-In Chicken Coop Design and Dimensions

The illustrated design features a solid timber frame, a pitched gable roof, heavy-gauge wire-mesh walls, a human walk-in access door, an elevated roosting coop, and an exterior nesting box array.

The main featured plan uses overall dimensions of 10 feet long × 8 feet wide × 8 feet high (305 × 244 × 244 cm).

Medium Setup (Featured)

8' W × 10' L × 8' H

(244 × 305 × 244 cm) — The sweet spot for most homesteaders. Offers full upright human walk-in height, separate roosting room, and comfortable space for 6 to 10 chickens.

Large Setup

10' W × 12' L × 8' H

(305 × 366 × 244 cm) — Heavy flock layout designed for 10 to 16 birds. Ample room for expanded nesting clusters, dual roost ladders, and integrated storage bins.

Important Planning Tip: Before finalizing your coop dimensions, always review municipal animal codes, HOA restrictions, and lot setback regulations. Most guidelines recommend a minimum of 4 sq. ft. of indoor coop space and 8–10 sq. ft. of outdoor run space per standard laying hen.

Hardware & Enclosure Materials:
  • 1/2-inch 19-gauge galvanized hardware cloth (avoid thin hex wire).
  • Corrugated black metal or polycarbonate roofing panels.
  • 3-inch and 2-1/2-inch exterior ceramic-coated star-drive screws.
  • Heavy-duty 8-inch T-hinges and spring-loaded lockable barrel latches.
  • Exterior ACX plywood for elevated coop walls and nesting lids.
Essential Carpenter Tools:
  • 25-ft tape measure, speed square, chalk line, and 4-ft spirit level.
  • Circular saw with fine framing blade, miter saw, and jigsaw.
  • Cordless drill and high-torque impact driver set.
  • Heavy-duty wire snips or pneumatic staple gun for hardware cloth.
  • Safety goggles, leather gloves, and hearing protection.

8-Step Step-by-Step Construction Guide

1

Prepare the Base Frame & Foundation

Select a well-drained, high-ground location in your yard. Lay out your 10' × 8' ground perimeter using ground-contact treated 4x4 or 2x6 timber skids. Verify squareness by measuring opposite diagonal corners (they must match exactly). Level the frame on concrete deck blocks or crushed gravel to keep wood elevated from moist soil.

2

Assemble Wall Studs & Vertical Uprights

Erect the four 8' vertical corner posts and horizontal top plates. Install intermediate 2x4 wall studs spaced every 24 inches on center. Frame out the rough opening for the full-height walk-in door (minimum 30" wide by 72" tall) on the front wall and leave framing bays for the elevated hen house on the rear section.

3

Install Roof Trusses & Structural Ridge Beam

Build matching 4-foot gable roof trusses using 2x4 rafters joined with plywood gusset plates. Hoist trusses onto the top wall plates spaced at 24" centers, tie them together with a central ridge board, and secure each rafter with metal Simpson hurricane ties to guarantee storm and high-wind resistance.

4

Install Pitched Corrugated Metal Roofing Panels

Attach 1x4 horizontal purlins across your rafters, then screw down black corrugated metal or polycarbonate roofing panels using neoprene-gasketed roofing screws. Provide a 6-inch overhang on all four sides to divert rainwater away from the wooden framework and keep the outdoor run dry.

5

Attach Predator-Resistant Wire Mesh (Hardware Cloth)

Wrap the entire run in 1/2-inch 19-gauge galvanized hardware cloth. Fasten the mesh with 1-inch pneumatic crown staples or screw-and-washer plates every 6 inches. Sandwich the wire edges beneath 1x3 wooden batten trim boards to prevent raccoons from prying the wire loose.

6

Construct Enclosed Roosting Coop, Nesting Boxes & Ramp

Sheathe the elevated 4' × 8' roosting coop with exterior plywood or shiplap siding. Mount an external 3-bay nesting box on the outside wall with a hinged, weather-sealed lid for easy egg retrieval. Install an angled wooden chicken ladder ramp with cleated rungs so hens can comfortably walk up into the coop at dusk.

7

Build & Hang the Walk-In Access Door

Construct a rigid 2x4 door frame with diagonal cross-braces to prevent sagging. Hang the door using three heavy-duty exterior T-hinges. Install a two-step predator-proof safety latch (such as a spring carabiner-locked barrel bolt) that curious raccoons cannot unlock.

8

Interior Fit-Out: Roost Bars, Feeders & Bedding

Install rounded 2x4 roosting bars (wide side flat for chicken feet protection during winter freezes) higher than the nesting boxes. Hang waterers and gravity feeders at chicken shoulder height to keep shavings out. Spread 4 inches of fresh kiln-dried pine shavings and introduce your happy flock!

Make the Coop Comfortable and Easy to Maintain

💨
High-Level Ridge Ventilation: Chickens release immense moisture and ammonia in their droppings. Provide continuous soffit and gable vents covered in hardware cloth near the roofline to vent humid air without blowing drafts directly onto sleeping birds.
🛡️
Buried Hardware Cloth Predator Apron: Predators like foxes and dogs attempt to dig underneath walls. Flare your 1/2" hardware cloth outward 12 to 18 inches flat along the ground (or bury it vertically 12" deep) and pin with turf staples.
☀️
Shade and Weather Protection: Overhangs and partial solid siding protect birds from harsh midday summer sun and lateral blowing winter sleet while maintaining high ambient daylight.

Final Pre-Flock Assembly Checklist

  • ✅ The base frame is 100% square, level, and anchored against wind uplift.
  • ✅ The metal roof is watertight with sealed purlin fasteners and generous drip overhangs.
  • ✅ Heavy-gauge 1/2" hardware cloth is secured with no gaps larger than a half-inch anywhere.
  • ✅ Walk-in and nesting box doors swing freely and lock with two-step predator latches.
  • ✅ All sharp screws, protruding wire ends, and rough splinters are countersunk and sanded.
  • ✅ Nesting boxes are clean, dark, private, and filled with fresh straw or wood shavings.
  • ✅ Roosting bars provide at least 10 inches of horizontal roost space per laying hen.
  • ✅ High gable vents allow continuous air exchange without cold winter drafts at roost level.
  • ✅ Feeders and fresh water fonts are elevated above bedding height to prevent contamination.
  • ✅ Local flock zoning regulations, rooster bans, and setbacks have been verified.

Frequently Asked Questions

How big should a walk-in chicken coop be?

For standard backyard laying breeds, plan for at least 3 to 4 square feet of indoor coop floor space per hen, plus 8 to 10 square feet of outdoor run space. A 10' × 8' walk-in footprint provides an ideal balance of flock comfort and easy human maintenance for 6 to 12 birds.

What is the best material for a chicken coop?

Pressure-treated lumber is recommended for the ground-contact base skids. For the upper walls and rafters, kiln-dried 2x4 Douglas fir or cedar provides structural rigidity without warping. Exterior ACX plywood or T1-11 siding paired with corrugated metal roofing creates a weather-tight shelter.

Is chicken wire enough to protect chickens from predators?

No. Chicken wire is designed solely to keep chickens in, not to keep predators out. Determined raccoons, foxes, and neighborhood dogs can easily rip through thin chicken wire. Always use heavy-gauge 1/2-inch welded galvanized hardware cloth secured with structural batten screws.

How many nesting boxes do I need for my flock?

A general rule is one nesting box (approx. 12" × 12" × 12") for every 3 to 4 hens. Hens share nesting boxes naturally throughout the day, so a 3-bay external nesting box will comfortably serve a flock of 8 to 12 laying birds.

How do I prevent foul coop odors and damp bedding?

Moisture is the root cause of ammonia smell. Build on high ground with a crushed stone base, provide continuous roofline ventilation, and practice the "deep litter method" using kiln-dried pine shavings or coarse hemp bedding. Clean droppings boards weekly.

Do I need a building permit to build a backyard chicken coop?

Requirements vary by city and county. In many jurisdictions, detached accessory structures under 100 to 120 square feet do not require a formal building permit, but zoning rules regarding property line setbacks (often 10–20 ft from property boundaries and 25–50 ft from neighboring dwellings) must be strictly respected.

Final Thoughts

Building your own walk-in chicken coop is one of the most rewarding backyard woodworking projects you can undertake. It provides your flock with a safe, healthy, and predator-resistant home while making daily chores like feeding, watering, and egg collecting an enjoyable routine.

Start with a design that matches your flock size and yard space, use durable exterior materials, and prioritize ventilation and predator protection above all else.
